About my coding life

My first exposure to technology was watching tv at 06:00 when I was a kid, but soon after I would start stealing my mothers iPad to watch youtube and pirate movies/series.

Fast forward a few years to the latter half of primary school and I get into dipping my toes in AutoHotKey, it began with rebinding keybinds for games. But eventually I did end up getting pretty good at being able to quickly put something hacky together whenever the need arose. Around this time I also learn of Python's existance, downloading it, making a single "Hello, World!" program and deciding that that was enough.

Later in high school I got back into programming again, this time to make datapacks and edit some mods for minecraft servers that I was playing on, learing a bit of Java and JSON formatting. That foray into Minecraft's backend somewhat resparked my wanting to program, and I ended up deciding to get back into Python, this time using the Pygame library (think of libraries as if they are processing classes filled with just a bunch of methods). I ended up remaking multiple games from my phone spending ~10 hours per game to do the coding and the spritework.

Then I come to my coding journey's dark age, where I learnt Ti-Basic. The language used by Texas Instruments' graphical calculators. Constantly running into hardware AND software limitations taught me a very different kind of coding, where I had to be extremely conservative resource-wise and needed to optimise the FUCK out of every single program I wrote. I must have made ~50 games on the calculator, but it had been reset multiple times in the exam period so they have sadly all been lost. I did learn very specific kinds of shorthands and techniques here, especially improving in debugging and solving for edgecases. Skills I still use now. All in all I am quite happy I did use it,

Now, here at UT, we've started with Processing, where I because of my previous experince learnt quickly. The gained knowledge of classes finally making my old confusion of java go away. (Thank you Marcus for being such a good teacher!) I personally think processing is kind of a weird choice by the UT, why not just teach us all javascript? It doesn't matter much, but still.
